Three ways to look like nothing else on the shelf.

You were right about the last round. It was competent and it was anonymous — soft cards, a gradient, a lavender-adjacent accent, an icon set anyone could have bought. That is the house style of every app shipped in the last eighteen months, and it buries the one thing your app has that no other Bible app has.

Your database already attributes every single word of scripture to the voice that spoke it — 780 distinct sources, colour-coded four ways, with word counts per story. The Narrator has 668,470 words. God has 279,815. Jesus has 82,433. Adam has 25. That is not a feature to put on a settings screen. That is the entire visual identity, and all three directions below are built out of it rather than decorated on top of it.

What was wrong
Generic surface treatment. Cards, radii and shadows doing the work that typography and structure should do. Nothing on screen was specific to scripture, let alone to your scripture.
What stays
The skeleton you approved. Five tabs, story list → story → react & note, cast, search, plans, saved, progress, you. Not one route changes.
What changes
The material. Each direction commits to one idea hard enough that a screenshot is recognisable at thumbnail size with the name cropped off.

DIRECTION 01

Rubric

The printed page, taken seriously. No cards, no corners, no shadows, no icons. Scripture has been set in type for five hundred years and it has never needed a rounded rectangle.

Ground

True white. Not cream, not off-white — the white of a page that has just come off press.

Type

Helvetica Neue set tight and large for titles and every label; Iowan Old Style for scripture itself. Two faces, no third.

Structure

Hairline rules only. A 62px source rail runs the full height of the reader — speakers live in the margin, in their ink, in small caps.

Motion

Almost none, and therefore felt. The drop cap draws itself in stroke by stroke. Tab underline slides. That is the whole budget.

The risk I’m taking here

Removing the chat bubbles. They were the WhatsApp anchor and they are comfortable, but they cap the app at “messaging app for the Bible” forever. Rubric bets that the margin rail reads faster, scales to 780 speakers without a tail-and-avatar problem, and looks like a hundred-pound object rather than a free download. If you want the bubbles kept, they belong in Direction 02 — where they earn their place.

DIRECTION 02

Cast

Scripture as an ensemble. Every voice owns a colour field, and the app changes colour depending on who is talking. Closer to a Criterion sleeve or an opera programme than to an app.

Ground

Oxblood for the shell, bone for the page, and a saturated field per voice. Colour is identity here, never decoration.

Type

Didot at poster scale for names and titles, deliberately cropped by the frame edge. Avenir Next Condensed in small caps for all data. Iowan for scripture.

Structure

Full-bleed fields. The reader keeps a 20px colour band down the left of each turn instead of a bubble — the same information, a tenth of the furniture.

Motion

The field cross-dissolves as you scroll between speakers, so the whole screen breathes in the colour of whoever holds the floor. One effect, used everywhere.

The risk I’m taking here

An oxblood-and-bone Bible app is a strong flavour, and a saturated field behind a character’s name will read as irreverent to some of your audience — particularly the red field behind Jesus. That is a real objection and you should test it before I build it. The upside is that no competitor can copy it without looking like they copied it.

DIRECTION 03

Thread

One continuous line runs through the entire app and never disappears. It is the spine of the story list; it becomes the margin of the reader; it coils into your year. Every transition is that one line changing shape.

Ground

Blue-black, near lightless, so the line is the only thing emitting. Everything else is set in bone at low weight.

Type

Futura / Avenir for numerals and labels — geometric, quiet, no personality of its own so the line keeps all of it. Iowan for scripture.

Structure

A 30px gutter reserved on every screen for the thread. Content sits to its right. Nothing else has a border.

Motion

The whole direction is one shared SVG path that never unmounts. Reanimated interpolates between path states on navigation, so the line visibly travels from list to reader to year. Everything else fades.

The risk I’m taking here

This one is the most expensive to build and the only one where the wow depends entirely on execution quality. A shared, morphing SVG path across navigation is real engineering — Reanimated 3 and react-native-svg are already in your dependencies, so it is possible, but budget roughly two weeks for the transition layer alone before any screen work. If that budget is not there, pick 01 or 02; a half-built Thread is worse than either.
