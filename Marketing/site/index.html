What you get

Stories, not chapters

Chapter numbers were added centuries after the fact and cut stories in half. The Bible is divided into 365 readings that end where the story ends.

Take a colour

Four people, one story, read out loud. Your turns light up and everything else dims, so you always know when to speak.

A page for every voice

Over 700 people speak. See how much each one says, which stories they appear in, and who they spoke with most.

Questions already written

Four discussion questions for every story, written three times over — for a family, a classroom and a small group.

A finish line you'll reach

Plans and challenges from seven stories to all 365, including one shaped around an academic year.

The year on one screen

Every story you finish fills in on a single map of the whole Bible, so progress is something you can look at rather than a number.

Reading it with a room

The app was built for the table and the classroom as much as for one person on a sofa. If you lead a group, the guide tells you everything you need — including what to hand your IT and safeguarding leads.

What it doesn't do

Worth knowing before you download it rather than after.

No audio

You do the reading. That is the format, but it is not a listen-along Bible.

No accounts, so no sync

Progress and notes stay on the device they were made on.

English only

French is planned, but is not in the current release.

One translation

The New Living Translation. It is the text, not a commentary.
