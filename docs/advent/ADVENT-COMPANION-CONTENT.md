## Day 1 · S365 · Rev 20:4-22:21

Intro: Many churches hear Jesus' call to stay alert (Mark 13) as Advent begins, and Revelation looks the same way, towards his return. The reading opens with a much-debated passage, so focus on chapters 21–22: a new heaven and a new earth, where God lives among his people. The Bible ends with a prayer: "Come, Lord Jesus!"

Key verses: Rev-22-20

Response: pray

Family:
1. These chapters describe a city with a river of life and no night. What would you most like to see there?
2. God promises to wipe every tear away. What makes people sad that you hope God will mend?
3. Who do you know who feels sad or hurt today? How could you cheer them up?
4. Pray together the words "Come, Lord Jesus!" and name one thing you hope God will make new.

School:
1. Which picture in Revelation 21–22 stands out to you most: the river, the tree of life, the city with no night? Why?
2. What hopes do the writer and his first readers seem to have for the world's future?
3. People of many beliefs long for a world without pain or injustice. What would you most want to change?
4. Take a quiet moment to pray, or simply to hope, for someone who is struggling. What would you wish for them?

Small group:
1. What details stand out in John's picture of the new heaven and new earth? Which part would you most want to experience?
2. What does this passage teach about what God is ultimately planning for his creation?
3. Where do you see brokenness, sadness or injustice around you? How does the promise of a renewed world speak into it?
4. Pray together for one place of brokenness you have named, and finish with the Bible's last prayer: "Come, Lord Jesus!"

Closing: Christian hope is not pretending everything is fine now; it is trusting that God will make all things new.

## Day 2 · S002 · Gen 3:1-5:32

Intro: The Christmas story begins long before Bethlehem. In Eden, humanity turns from God and the world is broken. Yet even here God speaks of an offspring of the woman who will strike the serpent's head (Genesis 3:15). Christians have traditionally seen this as the first glimpse of God's plan to rescue us through Jesus.

Key verses: Gen-3-15

Response: reconcile

Family:
1. Adam and Eve hid from God after they did wrong. When have you wanted to hide after making a mistake?
2. Even then, God made clothes for them. What does that tell you about how God treats people who mess up?
3. Cain was so angry with his brother Abel that he did something terrible. What can you do when you feel very angry with someone?
4. Is there anyone you need to say sorry to, or to forgive? Choose how you will do it today.

School:
1. What do the choices of the first humans in this story show about temptation, trust and wanting our own way?
2. How do their relationships with each other, with God and with the world change after the first wrong choice?
3. Where do we see broken trust, jealousy or selfishness in the world today? What helps people begin to put things right?
4. Is there a relationship in your life that needs repair? What small step towards putting it right could you take?

Small group:
1. What do Adam and Eve's choices reveal about temptation, trust and wanting to do things our own way?
2. In Genesis 3:15, God speaks of a future victory over evil. How does this help us see why the world needs a Saviour?
3. Where do you see the effects of broken relationships, selfishness or sin today? How do they touch our own lives?
4. Is there a relationship where you need to say sorry, forgive or make peace? What is one step you could take this week?

Closing: The story of hope begins in a garden where humanity turns away, but God does not abandon his creation.

## Day 3 · S006 · Gen 15:1-18:15

Intro: God promises Abraham descendants as many as the stars, and Abraham believes him. Later God gives him a new name and promises to make him "the father of a multitude of nations" (17:4). Abraham cannot yet see how this will happen. Matthew's Gospel begins with Abraham's family line. As you read, look for a promise bigger than one family.

Key verses: Gen-15-5

Response: act

Family:
1. God told Abraham to look up and count the stars. If you can, look at the sky tonight. How many can you count?
2. Abraham and Sarah were old, and they waited a long time for a son. What is something you have had to wait for?
3. Abraham believed God's promise even though he had questions. What question would you ask God?
4. God wanted Abraham to be a blessing. What kind thing could you do for someone outside your family this week?

School:
1. What does Abraham's conversation with God show about his doubts, questions and trust?
2. Abram becomes Abraham and Sarai becomes Sarah. Why might a new name matter at a turning point in someone's life?
3. What do you do when you have to wait for something that matters to you? What helps?
4. Think of someone outside your usual friends and family. What is one way you could be a blessing to them this week?

Small group:
1. What does Abraham's conversation with God reveal about his doubts, questions and faith?
2. Why is it significant that God's promise grows from a childless couple into "a multitude of nations" (17:4)?
3. Is there something you are finding hard to trust God with at the moment? What might Abraham's story teach you about waiting?
4. How could you become a source of blessing to someone outside your usual circle of family and friends this week?

Closing: God's promises are often bigger than we can see at the time, and hope grows as we learn to trust the One who makes them.

## Day 4 · S008 · Gen 20:1-23:20

Intro: God asks Abraham to offer his son Isaac, the child of the promise. Abraham trusts, saying "God will provide," and God provides a ram in Isaac's place. Afterwards God promises that through Abraham's family all nations will be blessed (22:18). Christians have long seen this story as a picture of God's provision, pointing towards Jesus.

Key verses: Gen-22-8

Response: give thanks

Family:
1. Isaac asked where the lamb for the offering was. What did Abraham answer?
2. In the end, what did God provide in Isaac's place?
3. When has someone given you exactly what you needed at just the right time?
4. Say thank you together for three things you were given this week. Thank whoever gave them, and thank God if you wish.

School:
1. What do you find most difficult or surprising about this story of Abraham and Isaac?
2. What does the ram in the thicket show about how the story sees God?
3. What is the difference between trusting when life is going well and trusting when we do not understand?
4. Who has provided for you or supported you in a hard time? How could you thank them this week?

Small group:
1. What do you find most difficult or surprising about Abraham's experience in this passage?
2. What does God's provision of the ram reveal about him?
3. How does trusting God when life is going well differ from trusting him when we do not understand what is happening?
4. Give thanks together for one way God has provided for you, big or small. Who could you thank for being there for you this week?

Closing: Abraham discovered that God provides, and at Christmas Christians celebrate God's greatest provision: his Son.

## Day 5 · S085 · Rut 1:1-4:22

Intro: Ruth is a young widow from Moab who stays with her mother-in-law Naomi and trusts the God of Israel. She marries Boaz and becomes the great-grandmother of King David. A Moabite woman appears in the family line of Jesus (Matthew 1:5). God's promises reach further than we might expect.

Key verses: Rut-4-17

Response: act

Family:
1. Ruth told Naomi, "Wherever you go, I will go." What does that tell you about Ruth?
2. Boaz let Ruth gather grain from his field and made sure she was safe. How did he show kindness?
3. Who might feel new, left out or alone at your school or in your street?
4. Choose one person to include or help this week. What will you do?

School:
1. What stands out to you about Ruth's loyalty to Naomi? What does her decision cost her?
2. How does Boaz treat Ruth? What does everyday kindness look like in this story?
3. Ruth is an outsider in Israel. Who might feel like an outsider where you live, study or work?
4. Is there someone you could include, encourage or help this week? What could that look like?

Small group:
1. What stands out to you about Ruth's loyalty to Naomi? What does her decision cost her?
2. How does the way Boaz treats Ruth show kindness and generosity?
3. Who might feel like an outsider, overlooked or alone in your community? What might this story teach us about welcoming them?
4. Is there someone you could intentionally include, encourage or help this week? What could that look like?

Closing: God's family is bigger than we sometimes imagine, and faithful love can change lives.

## Day 6 · S090 · 1Sa 15:1-16:23

Intro: Israel's first king, Saul, has failed to follow God. Samuel is sent to Bethlehem, to Jesse's family, and God chooses David, the youngest, a shepherd overlooked by his own family. God says, "the Lord looks at the heart." Matthew's Gospel later calls Jesus a descendant of David. Notice how God sees people.

Key verses: 1Sa-16-1

Response: notice

Family:
1. David was the youngest, and he was out looking after the sheep. Why do you think nobody thought to call him at first?
2. God said, "People judge by outward appearance, but the Lord looks at the heart." What do you think that means?
3. Why is it easy to judge people by how they look?
4. Look for someone this week who is often overlooked. What good thing do you notice about them?

School:
1. What differences do you notice between Saul and David in this passage? What do their actions suggest about their hearts?
2. Why might Samuel have expected a different choice? What does the story say about how people decide who matters?
3. When do we put too much weight on appearance, popularity, ability or status?
4. Whose gifts or character might be overlooked around you? Take time this week to notice them and name something good.

Small group:
1. What differences do you notice between Saul and David? What do their behaviour and choices reveal about their hearts?
2. Why does God tell Samuel not to judge by appearance? What does this teach us about the way God sees people?
3. How can we put too much importance on appearance, popularity, ability or status when deciding who matters?
4. Who might be overlooked around you? Ask God to help you notice them this week, and tell them what you see in them.

Closing: God's choice of David reminds us that outward appearance is not what matters most, and the promised King came in a way many did not expect.

## Day 7 · S191 · Isa 6:1-9:7

Intro: Isaiah sees God's holiness and answers, "Here I am. Send me." Later King Ahaz is terrified by enemy armies, and Isaiah gives him a sign: a child named Immanuel, "God is with us". Matthew applies this promise to Jesus (1:23). Isaiah also points forward to a child who will rule on David's throne (9:6–7).

Key verses: Isa-7-14, Isa-9-6, Isa-9-7

Response: pray

Family:
1. Isaiah heard God ask, "Who will go for us?" What did Isaiah say?
2. Immanuel means "God is with us." When is it a comfort to know that someone is with you?
3. King Ahaz was very scared. What makes you feel scared sometimes, and what helps?
4. Pray together for someone who feels scared or alone, and ask God to be with them.

School:
1. What do Isaiah's vision and reply show about how he saw God and his own place?
2. King Ahaz is frightened of enemy armies when Isaiah offers him a sign. What do you notice about how Ahaz responds?
3. What would the name Immanuel, "God is with us", have meant to people living through danger? Where might people feel afraid or alone today?
4. Take a quiet moment to pray, or simply to hope, for someone who feels alone. What would you want them to know?

Small group:
1. What do Isaiah's vision and response reveal about God's holiness and Isaiah's willingness to serve?
2. What would "God is with us" have meant to frightened King Ahaz? How does Matthew later apply the promise to Jesus?
3. Where might people today feel fearful, uncertain or alone? How could the promise that God is with us speak to them?
4. Pray together for someone who feels alone, and ask whether God is inviting you to answer like Isaiah: "Here I am. Send me."

Closing: The promise of Immanuel is at the heart of Christmas: God did not stay distant from our struggles, but came near.

## Day 8 · S199 · Isa 40:1-41:29

Intro: God speaks to his people in exile: "Comfort, comfort my people." A voice calls them to prepare the way in the wilderness (40:3). All four Gospels link that voice with John the Baptist, who prepares the way for Jesus. As the second week begins, ask what it means to make room for the coming King.

Key verses: Isa-40-1, Isa-40-3

Response: give thanks

Family:
1. God tells his people, "Comfort, comfort my people." What helps you feel better when you are sad?
2. The reading says that grass dies and flowers fade, but "the word of our God stands forever." What lasts in your life?
3. Who comforted you the last time you were upset?
4. Say thank you for a time you were comforted, and thank the person who did it.

School:
1. Which words or images here would have brought comfort to people who felt forgotten or far from home?
2. What does Isaiah say about the greatness and faithfulness of God compared with the things people trust instead?
3. What do people today rely on for security, identity or peace? Which of those things feel strong, and which feel fragile?
4. Who has comforted or encouraged you recently? Find a way to thank them this week.

Small group:
1. Which words or images would have brought comfort to people who felt forgotten or far from home?
2. What does Isaiah say about the greatness and faithfulness of God compared with the things people trust instead?
3. What things do people depend on for security, identity or peace that cannot ultimately give what they need?
4. Give thanks together for a time God comforted you. Who might need to hear a word of comfort from you this week?

Closing: God's comfort does not mean every difficulty vanishes at once; it means we can trust his presence, power and faithfulness while we wait.

## Day 9 · S257 · Mic 4:9-7:20

Intro: Micah warns of judgement but also promises restoration. A ruler will come from Bethlehem, "only a small village among all the people of Judah" (5:2). When Herod asks where the Messiah will be born, the experts point to this promise (Matthew 2:5–6). God's King comes from an unexpected place, to lead and to bring peace.

Key verses: Mic-5-2

Response: reconcile

Family:
1. Micah says a ruler will come from Bethlehem, a small village. Why is it surprising that a king would come from a small place?
2. Micah says God delights in showing "unfailing love". What does it mean to forgive someone?
3. When has someone forgiven you? How did it feel?
4. Is there someone you have argued with? Think of one kind thing you could say or do to make peace.

School:
1. What different emotions do you notice in Micah's words? Where do you see both honesty about suffering and hope?
2. Why might it matter that the promised ruler comes from a small town like Bethlehem?
3. Micah speaks of God forgiving and showing mercy. How should people respond when they have failed, or when others have let them down?
4. Is there a relationship where peace or forgiveness is needed? What first step could you take this week?

Small group:
1. What different emotions do you notice in Micah's words? Where do you see both honesty about suffering and confidence in God?
2. Why is it significant that God's promised ruler will come from Bethlehem, a seemingly unimportant town?
3. What does Micah's confidence in God's mercy teach us about how to respond when we have failed or when others have let us down?
4. Is there a relationship that needs peace, forgiveness or reconciliation? Ask God for courage, and decide on one step this week.

Closing: God's mercy is greater than our failure, and the promised Shepherd-King comes to lead and restore his people.

## Day 10 · S200 · Isa 42:1-44:23

Intro: God's chosen servant will bring justice to the nations, and gently: "He will not crush the weakest reed or put out a flickering candle." In Isaiah the servant is Israel, and also one who fulfils Israel's calling. Matthew applies this passage to Jesus (Matthew 12). Notice what kind of strength God promises.

Key verses: Isa-42-3

Family:

2. Is the servant loud and pushy, or gentle? How can you tell?
3. Who in your class or family is like a flickering candle, needing a little care?
4. Look out this week for someone who feels weak or left out. What do you notice about them, and how could you be gentle with them?

School:
1. What kind of person does Isaiah describe when he speaks about God's servant? Which details stand out?
2. How is the servant's way of bringing justice different from the ways people often seek power or control?
3. Who in our communities might feel like the weakest reed or a flickering candle? What would gentle strength look like?
4. Over the next few days, notice someone who is struggling. What would it look like to treat them gently?

Small group:
1. What kind of person does Isaiah describe when he speaks about God's servant? Which details stand out?
2. How does the servant's way of bringing justice differ from the ways people often try to gain power or control?
3. Who might feel like the weakest reed or a flickering candle in our communities? What might it mean to treat them as Jesus would?
4. Ask God to help you notice someone who is struggling this week. What might you say or do when you see them?

Closing: Jesus shows that true strength does not need to dominate; God's servant brings justice with gentleness, faithfulness and compassion.

## Day 12 · S203 · Isa 52:13-57:21

Intro: Isaiah describes a servant who suffers for others: "he was pierced for our rebellion, crushed for our sins." Christians have long seen this as a picture of Jesus' suffering and death. Advent leads to the manger, but the child of Bethlehem also points towards the cross. It is a heavy reading, so take it slowly.

Key verses: Isa-53-5

Response: pray

Family:
1. Isaiah describes someone who was hurt so that others could be whole. Who has helped you even when it cost them something?
2. Isaiah says, "All of us, like sheep, have strayed away." What happens when a sheep wanders off? Who comes to find it?
3. When have you needed help to carry something heavy, such as a bag, or a worry?
4. Pray together for someone who is carrying something heavy right now. Name them out loud.

School:
1. Which words or details in this description of a suffering servant are difficult or powerful for you?
2. Jews and Christians have read this passage in different ways. What do you notice in the text itself that might shape those readings?
3. What does it mean to care for others at a cost to yourself, rather than only when it is convenient?
4. Take a quiet moment to pray, or hold in mind, someone carrying a burden. What practical help could you offer them?

Small group:
1. What words or details in Isaiah's description of the suffering servant are difficult or particularly powerful?
2. How does this passage help Christians understand the purpose of Jesus' life and death?
3. What does it mean to love others sacrificially rather than only when it is convenient?
4. Pray together for someone carrying a burden, and ask God what practical kindness, patience or service you could offer them.

Closing: The Christmas story does not end at the manger; Jesus came to enter our suffering and make a way for us to be restored to God.

## Day 13 · S215 · Jer 29:1-31:40

Jeremiah also looks ahead to a new covenant, with God's instructions written on hearts (31:33). Christians see this new covenant in Jesus.

School:

2. What is the difference between the hope God offers in this passage and simply expecting life to be easy?
3. Jeremiah tells the exiles to "work for the peace and prosperity of the city" where they are. What might that look like for us, even when things are not as we hoped?

Small group:
1. What instructions does Jeremiah give the people living in exile? What might have surprised them about God's message?
2. What is the difference between the hope God offers in this passage and simply expecting life to become easy?

4. What could you do this week to contribute to the wellbeing of your family, school, workplace or local community?

Closing: God's promise was spoken to people in a difficult season, and hope can grow even while we wait.

## Day 14 · S238 · Eze 34:1-36:38

Small group:

4. Who in your life might need a good shepherd? Ask God to help you notice them this week, and think how you could listen, protect, encourage or help.

Closing: God does not forget people who have been neglected, and Jesus comes as the Good Shepherd, inviting us to care for others in the same way.

2. Paul says to "pray about everything" and to "thank him for all he has done". What would you thank God for tonight?
3. Paul says to think of others as better than yourselves. How can you show someone that they matter?
4. Say thank you today to three people for something specific, and thank God too if you wish.

School:
