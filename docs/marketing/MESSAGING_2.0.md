## 1 · The one idea

Everything below is a way of saying one thing:

> **Every word in the Bible was said by someone. This app never lets you forget who.**

That is *attribution*, and attribution is the product. It is not a reading theme, a font, or a colour scheme
laid over a study Bible — it is a re-typesetting of the whole biblical narrative around the question
*who is speaking right now?*, done 774 times across 365 stories.

**Master line (use everywhere):**

```
A Bible you read out loud, by voice.
```

**Support line (the four facts that make it concrete):**

```
774 voices. 365 stories. Four source colours. About fifteen minutes a day.
```

**Elevator version (30 seconds, for podcasts, pastors, school heads):**

> Most people meet the Bible as a wall of text and lose track of who is talking. SourceView Together
> attributes every word to whoever said it — the narrator, God, the named people at the centre of the story,
> and everyone else — each in its own colour. The Bible is laid out as 365 complete stories instead of
> chapters, so you read a whole arc in about fifteen minutes. And because every voice is marked, a family or
> a small group can open the same story, each take a colour, and read it aloud together. It's free, it works
> offline, and nothing you do in it leaves your phone.

## 3 · What is genuinely new since 1.x — and how to say it

The 1.x app was a Bible with colour-coded speech. The 2.0 app is a different product shape. Frame the
upgrade as **"the Bible you already had, now with a way through it and people to meet."**

| Area | 1.x | 2.0 | Post-ready framing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Navigation | Book/chapter lists and cards | **The thread** — ten divisions, one continuous path, story colour boxes when you open one | "We threw out the menu. The Bible is one path now." |
| People | Speakers were colours | **Cast** — 774 named voices, opening on God and Jesus, with counts and appearances | "Meet the cast." |
| Group reading | QR codes, sessions, coordination screens | **Nothing to set up** — open the same story, take a colour, read | "We deleted the group feature. Group reading got better." |
| Onboarding | Straight into text | Teach the four colours once, then open today's story | "Ten seconds of teaching, then you're reading." |
| Reflection | Achievement badges | **The You tab** — heatmap, rings, streak, word mix, voices met, next voices, thin eras | "Your reading, honestly reported." |
| Plans | Fixed catalogue | Catalogue **plus custom plans** you build like a playlist | "Build your own plan. Every story David speaks in, in one list." |
| Search | Limited | Voices, books, stories and references across all 66 books, landing on the actual verse | "Search a person, not just a word." |
| Craft | — | Dark mode and text size everywhere, verse-accurate search, crash reporting, faster launch | "A hundred small things you'll never notice, which is the point." |

**The single best "what's new" sentence:** *"Version 2.0 turned a colour-coded Bible into a year you can
walk, with 774 people to meet along the way."*

## 4 · Audience messaging

Your original brief cut off at "different and better for…". Here are the five audiences the product actually
serves, in the order I'd spend money and effort on them. Pick one to lead with and the rest become
supporting content — tell me which and I'll cut the rest down to size.

### A · Families at the table *(strongest fit — lead here)*
- **Pain:** "Family devotions" collapses because one person reads and everyone else waits.
- **Promise:** Everyone has a part in under a minute, and the questions afterwards are already written.
- **Hooks:** "Give the seven-year-old a colour." · "The first family Bible time nobody had to prepare."
  · "Fifteen minutes, four voices, one story, then three questions."
- **CTA:** Open tonight's story, hand out the colours, read one story out loud.

### B · Small groups & home churches
- **Pain:** Silence after the reading; the leader carries everything.
- **Promise:** A whole story instead of a fragment, everyone reading, questions written for small groups.
- **Hooks:** "Nobody has to prepare." · "The person who never talks has a colour now." · "Read the whole
  story. Then talk about the whole story."
- **CTA:** Same story, phones out, take a colour, twenty minutes.

### C · Christian schools, chapel & youth groups
- **Pain:** Getting teenagers to engage with a text they find impenetrable and socially awkward to read aloud.
- **Promise:** Reading parts is a low-risk way to speak in a room; school-specific questions; the Bible in
  180 stories fits an academic year.
- **Hooks:** "Bible in one school year — 180 stories." · "Four students, four colours, one story, one period."
  · "Questions written for a classroom, not a pulpit."
- **CTA:** One story per chapel or form time; rotate colours weekly.

### D · The lapsed one-year-plan reader
- **Pain:** They've started a reading plan in January three times and quit in Leviticus.
- **Promise:** Complete stories, not daily quotas; a thread that shows where you are; a streak on your own
  calendar; pause and resume without guilt.
- **Hooks:** "You didn't fail the plan. The plan was shaped wrong." · "Finish something today."
- **CTA:** Start with one story. Not the year.

### E · Curious / returning readers
- **Pain:** "I open it and I can't tell who's talking or why any of this is happening."
- **Promise:** Attribution removes the confusion; a story is a self-contained fifteen minutes.
- **Hooks:** "If you've ever lost the thread of who's talking — this is the fix." · "Start with a story,
  not a chapter."
- **CTA:** Read the first story. That's the whole ask.

## 7 · Language rules

**Always**
- *source colours* (never "ink", never "roles" in user-facing copy)
- *voices* and *Cast* for people; *stories* for the 365 segments; *the thread* for the year
- *Talk about it* for the post-story questions
- "read out loud", "take a colour", "meet a voice"
- British/US spelling: pick one per market and hold it (store copy currently uses "colors" — match it)

**Never**
- Any comparison to another Bible app, named or implied ("unlike other apps", "most Bible apps…")
- "Gamified", "engagement", "content" — three words that make Scripture sound like a feed
- Claims about the number of users, downloads or churches until you can cite them
- Feature names that no longer exist: sessions, QR codes, group codes, roles

## 8 · Creative direction

- **The screen is the hero.** Real text, real colours, big type, slow scroll. No stock photos of open Bibles
  on a table with coffee. The whole pitch is visible in three seconds of a real screen recording.
- **Sound matters.** This is an app about reading aloud — use real voices, several of them, imperfect and
  overlapping. Silence + captions for feed; real audio for Reels and TikTok.
- **Show hands and rooms, not faces in prayer.** Four hands holding four phones around one table is the
  category image nobody else owns.
- **Colour is the brand.** Every asset should be immediately identifiable by the four source colours before
  a single word is read.
- **One screenshot set per audience.** Family, small group, school, solo — the same app, four first screens.
