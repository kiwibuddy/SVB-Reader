### Full description — 3,078 / 4,000

```
SourceView Together is a Bible you read out loud, by voice.

Every word is attributed to whoever said it. Narration, divine speech, the named people at the center of each story, and everyone else each read in their own source color, with the speaker's name beside their words. You always know who is talking.


READ WHOLE STORIES

The Bible is laid out as 365 complete stories with real beginnings and endings, not chapters. Most take about fifteen minutes read out loud. Follow the year on a single thread that runs from Genesis to Revelation through ten divisions, so you can always see where you are.


READ TOGETHER

Open the same story, take a source color each, and read that voice aloud. There is nothing to set up: no host, no invitations, no accounts. The colors do the coordinating. It works the same for four people at a kitchen table and four students at the front of a class.


TALK ABOUT IT

Every story ends with four discussion questions, written three ways — for families, for schools, and for small groups. Over four thousand questions in each language, so nobody has to prepare anything.


MEET THE CAST

773 distinct voices speak across the 365 stories. Cast shows who you have heard, how often they speak, where they appear, who they spoke with, and their longest exchange. Abraham's is eight turns, with God, in Sodom & Gomorrah.


REACT AND REMEMBER

Add a reaction or a note to a whole turn of speech rather than a fragment of a verse, so what you keep holds on to its speaker and its context. Saved gathers them in one place.


READING PLANS

Twelve plans and challenges are built in: Bible in 1 Year, Bible in 1 School Year (180 stories), New Testament in 100 Days, The Gospels, Paul's Letters, Women of the Bible, David's Life, In the Beginning, Old and New Testament Journeys, Advent, Lent and Christmas. New in version 2.0, you can build your own plan from any stories, whole books, or every story one voice appears in.


YOUR YEAR

The You tab shows a reading heatmap, a streak that follows your own calendar, your word mix by source color, the voices you have met, the voices you have not, and the eras you have read thin. Pause a plan when life is loud and resume without losing your place.


ENGLISH AND FRENCH

Complete French throughout: Bible text, discussion questions and interface.


FREE

No ads, no subscriptions, no in-app purchases, no account.


PRIVATE AND OFFLINE

Reading progress, reactions, notes and plans stay in a database on your device. Reading works with no network at all. Anonymous crash reports help us fix problems; nothing else is sent.


MADE FOR

Families who want fifteen minutes at the table. Small groups and home churches. Youth groups, chapels and Christian schools. Solo readers who would rather finish a story than a chapter. Anyone who has opened a Bible and lost track of who was talking.


New Living Translation, chosen because it reads well out loud. Dark mode and adjustable text size. Optional daily reminders, scheduled on your device. Phones and tablets.

Open the first story and see who is talking.
```

## What changed from the version 1 listing, and why

| Version 1 said | Version 2 says | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Transform Your Bible Reading Experience”, “Read the Bible like never before!” | Plain statements of what the app does | The voice is quiet and exact. Hype and exclamation marks are off-brand and they age badly |
| Emoji section markers throughout | Plain capitalised headers | Still scannable in the Play layout, without looking like a flyer |
| “Host or join group reading sessions”, “Share with QR codes” | “Open the same story, take a source color each” | The QR codes, host/join handshake and role-selection screens were deleted in 2.0. Describing them would be describing an app that no longer exists |
| “Assign up to 4 reading roles” | “Take a source colour” | *Roles* is retired vocabulary; the colours are the assignment |
| “Real-time emoji reactions as you read together” | “Add a reaction or a note to a whole turn of speech” | Reactions are personal and stored on the device — they are not shared live between readers. The old line promised something the app does not do |
| “Add notes to any Bible verse” | “…to a whole turn of speech rather than a fragment of a verse” | Reactions and notes attach to a speech block (`emojis` table: `segmentID` + `blockID`), which is the more distinctive behaviour anyway |
| “Complete Bible text” | “365 complete stories … from Genesis to Revelation” | Accurate, and it is the stronger claim |
| “400+ study questions” | “Over four thousand questions in each language” | The real figure: 4 questions × 365 stories × 3 audiences, plus a second set covering 345 stories for family and school |
| “Achievement system”, “Challenge milestones” | The You tab: heatmap, streak, word mix, voices met, thin eras | The badges page was folded into You in 2.0 |
| “We don't sell information or track your reading habits” | “…stay in a database on your device … Anonymous crash reports help us fix problems; nothing else is sent” | Precise, and it accounts for Sentry. A privacy claim that overstates is the one claim you cannot recover from |
| Nothing about Cast, the thread, custom plans, or search | All four have their own sections | These are the 2.0 release |

## Before you paste

- **Decide 773 or 774 voices** and use the same figure here, in the Apple listing and in
  `APP_DESCRIPTION.md`. This copy says 773, which is what the shipped data counts.
- **US or UK spelling.** This copy uses *color* to match the existing Play listing. If the listing is
  en-GB, change five instances to *colour*.
- **Name the French Bible translation** in the French listing, with whatever attribution its licence
  requires. The repo doesn't record which translation `FRA-Bible.json` is, so the line is left out
  rather than guessed.
- **Apple's listing needs the same pass** — `docs/store/STORE_LISTING_1.3.0.md` is still 1.3.0 copy, and
  its subtitle, promotional text, keywords and What's New have not been updated for 2.0.
- Screenshots and the feature graphic still show the 1.x screens. New captures should lead with an
  attributed passage, the thread, and Cast.
